## What It Is

Thymosin Alpha-1: a 28-amino-acid peptide (sold under the brand name Zadaxin) that comes from the thymus gland and broadly fine-tunes the immune system. Thymosin Beta-4: a 43-amino-acid peptide that your body releases after injury to help tissues repair themselves.

What it does (Thymosin Alpha-1): This peptide acts like a master controller of your immune defenses — it wakes up multiple types of immune cells (including the ones that fight viruses and the ones that recognize and destroy infected cells), helps your body recognize and fight threats more effectively, and can even dial down overactive immune responses that cause damage. What it does (Thymosin Beta-4): After you're injured, your body releases this peptide from blood platelets and immune cells. It acts like a repair crew foreman — attracting stem cells to the injury site, triggering the growth of new blood vessels, telling cells to migrate to where they're needed, and tamping down scar formation so tissue heals cleanly rather than leaving excessive scarring.

## Potential Benefits

- Thymosin Alpha-1 is approved in 35+ countries to treat chronic hepatitis B and C
- May improve outcomes in serious infections including sepsis and COVID-19
- Research suggests it could boost the effectiveness of cancer treatments like chemotherapy
- Thymosin Beta-4 could accelerate healing of skin wounds, pressure sores, and eye injuries
- May protect the heart after a heart attack and reduce permanent damage
- Could reduce scar tissue formation in lungs and liver
- Research suggests potential for stimulating hair growth

## Where It Came From

Thymosin Alpha-1 was first isolated from calf thymus gland extracts in the early 1960s by Abraham White at Albert Einstein College of Medicine, New York, with the full sequence published in 1977 by Allan Goldstein (University of Texas). Thymosin Beta-4 was fully characterized in 1981 by Hannappel and colleagues at the National Cancer Institute in Bethesda, USA.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

### THYMOSIN ALPHA-1

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (most effective, standard route)**

Nearly all clinical studies use subcutaneous injection. The peptide is absorbed reliably and reaches immune cells effectively. This is the standard route used in approved products (e.g., Zadaxin) worldwide.

**💉 Intramuscular injection / into the muscle (used in some clinical settings)**

Some COVID-19 protocols used IM injection; absorption is similar to subcutaneous and may be slightly faster, but subcutaneous is preferred for ease of self-administration.

### THYMOSIN BETA-4 / TB-500

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (standard and most practical)**

The primary route used in research and clinical protocols. Good bioavailability and easy to self-administer.

**💉 Intramuscular injection / into the muscle (used for acute injury protocols)**

Some practitioners prefer IM for faster delivery to injured muscle tissue, particularly in acute injury settings.

### 🏥 Intravenous / IV (used in clinical trials only)

Phase I safety studies used IV administration for Thymosin Beta-4. This gives near-100% delivery but requires medical supervision and is not for self-use.

### 🧴 Topical / on the skin (limited effectiveness)

Eye drops containing Thymosin Beta-4 (RGN-259) have shown benefit in corneal healing. For systemic effects like muscle repair, topical application on intact skin does not work because the peptide molecule is too large to pass through skin.

## Dosage Context

**THYMOSIN ALPHA-1**

- Lower end of the reported range: 0.5–1 mg subcutaneously, once daily or a few times per week. Used for general immune maintenance or low-level chronic stress support.
- Typical range reported in the literature: 1.6 mg subcutaneously, twice per week. This is the most common dose in published clinical trials for conditions like hepatitis B, HIV, and immune recovery.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: 3.2–6.4 mg administered per week (e.g., 1.6 mg four times per week), or in intensive acute-illness protocols, daily dosing at 1.6 mg for 7–14 days. Higher single doses have been studied in critical care and cancer settings.

**THYMOSIN BETA-4 / TB-500**

- Lower end of the reported range: 300–500 mcg (micrograms) daily, subcutaneous.
- Typical range reported in the literature: 300 mcg to 1 mg administered daily for immune/recovery use; up to 5 mg twice weekly for acute injury loading phases.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: 5 mg twice weekly during a 4–6 week loading phase for acute tissue repair. Long-term use should not exceed 3 months without a break.

## Timing & Cycling

THYMOSIN ALPHA-1: Most studies administer in the morning, aligning with the body's natural cortisol and immune activity peaks. Consistency of timing matters more than the exact hour. For twice-weekly dosing, spread injections evenly (e.g., Monday and Thursday).

THYMOSIN BETA-4: Can be taken at any time of day. For injury recovery, some practitioners time it post-workout or close to therapy sessions when tissue repair signals are highest. Daily use should be at a consistent time. There are no food restrictions.

THYMOSIN ALPHA-1: Most practitioners cycle it — a common pattern is 3 months on, 1 month off, repeated as needed. For chronic conditions, some use 6 months on, 2 months off. Acute loading protocols run 7–20 days continuously. Long-term continuous use is possible under physician supervision but cycling is generally preferred to prevent receptor desensitization and allow reassessment of immune function.

THYMOSIN BETA-4: Cycling is required. The consensus is no more than 3 months of continuous use. Common patterns are 3 months on / 6 weeks off, or 6 weeks on / 6 weeks off for long-term protocols. This prevents potential over-stimulation of blood vessel growth and allows the body to normalize.

Important: Thymosin Alpha-1 (Tα1) / Thymosin Beta-4 (Tβ4) is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
