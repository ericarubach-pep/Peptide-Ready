## What It Is

Teriparatide (rhPTH 1-34) — a 34-amino-acid lab-made fragment of the parathyroid hormone (a hormone naturally produced by four small glands in your neck that regulates calcium and bone strength)

## What It Does

Normally, your parathyroid hormone constantly circulating in the blood actually breaks down bone over time (to release calcium). But when this hormone is given as a brief daily injection — mimicking a short pulse rather than constant exposure — it flips the effect entirely: instead of breaking bone down, it stimulates the bone-building cells (called osteoblasts) to work harder, creating new bone tissue. Think of it like the difference between a sprint and a slow jog — the same hormone, but timed differently, produces opposite results.

## Potential Benefits

- Increases bone density at the spine by 8–13% and at the hip by 3–6%
- Reduces the risk of spine fractures by approximately 65% in postmenopausal women with prior fractures
- Reduces the risk of other bone fractures by approximately 53%
- FDA-approved (2002) for osteoporosis in postmenopausal women, men, and people on long-term steroids
- The first-ever approved treatment that actually builds new bone (rather than just slowing bone loss)
- Also being studied for speeding up fracture healing and treating bone loss in other conditions

## Where It Came From

Parathyroid hormone was first isolated by James Collip in Canada in 1925; the active 1–34 fragment was characterized in the 1970s in the USA, and Eli Lilly developed the recombinant version, which received FDA approval in November 2002 under the brand name Forteo after a landmark clinical trial published in 2001.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (only approved and established route)**

Teriparatide (brand name Forteo) is an FDA-approved osteoporosis drug administered exclusively via subcutaneous injection, once daily into the thigh or abdomen. The pre-filled injection pen makes self-administration straightforward. This is the only route supported by clinical evidence.

### 💊 Oral / by mouth (not available

being researched) — Teriparatide as a 34-amino acid peptide cannot survive oral digestion in its current form. Oral delivery research is ongoing but not yet available in any approved product.

### 💉 IV or IM (not used in clinical practice)

Intravenous or intramuscular routes have been studied in limited research contexts but are not part of any approved or standard protocol.

## Dosage Context

- Standard dose: 20 mcg once daily subcutaneously. This is the only FDA-approved dose for all indications — postmenopausal osteoporosis, male osteoporosis, and glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis. There is no beginner/intermediate/advanced tier for teriparatide because it is a prescription medication with a fixed approved dose. Body weight does not change the dose. A higher dose (40 mcg/day) was studied but was not adopted due to concerns about osteosarcoma risk (observed in rats at very high long-term doses, though not confirmed in humans at clinical doses).

## Timing & Cycling

Teriparatide is injected once daily at the same time each day. While timing is flexible (morning or evening), consistency is the key. Recent research (2025) has explored whether morning vs. evening dosing affects bone formation outcomes, but no definitively superior time has been established in clinical guidelines. Most patients simply pick a time they can remember (often morning) and stick with it. It can be given with or without food. Storage: the pre-filled pen must be kept refrigerated.

Teriparatide has a hard, mandatory treatment limit: a maximum cumulative lifetime use of 2 years (24 months) total. This is an FDA requirement, not a cycling choice, driven by findings of osteosarcoma (bone tumors) in rats given very high long-term doses. Within those 2 years, it is taken every day without breaks. After completing teriparatide, patients are typically transitioned to an antiresorptive medication (such as a bisphosphonate or denosumab) to preserve the bone density gains, since the new bone built during teriparatide treatment can be lost fairly quickly if no follow-up therapy is used.

Important: Teriparatide (rhPTH 1-34) is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
