## What It Is

TB-500 (fequesetide) — a 7-amino-acid synthetic fragment taken from the middle section of a naturally occurring repair protein called Thymosin Beta-4. It's the part of that protein responsible for most of its tissue-healing properties.

## What It Does

Every cell in your body has an internal skeleton made of a protein called actin — think of it like the scaffolding inside a building. TB-500 works by regulating how actin is assembled and disassembled, which allows cells to move toward injury sites, close wounds, and build new tissue. It also triggers the growth of new small blood vessels (so repaired tissue gets the blood supply it needs), reduces local inflammation, and sends signals that protect cells from dying unnecessarily. Essentially it activates your body's own repair crew.

## Potential Benefits

- Promoted for faster recovery from muscle, tendon, and ligament injuries
- May accelerate skin wound healing and reduce scar tissue formation
- Research on the full parent protein (Thymosin Beta-4) showed acceleration of hard-to-heal skin wounds and pressure ulcers in clinical trials
- Could support heart muscle recovery after a heart attack
- Research suggests potential for relief of dry eye and corneal injuries
- Early evidence hints at possible hair growth stimulation

## Where It Came From

The specific 7-amino-acid sequence at the core of TB-500 was first identified as biologically active by Hannappel and colleagues in the early 1990s. A key 2003 mouse wound-healing study by Philp and colleagues confirmed that this exact fragment could drive tissue repair on its own, establishing the basis for TB-500 as a standalone compound.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

### 💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (standard route)

The most widely used and recommended route. TB-500 is injected into subcutaneous fat (belly, thighs, upper arms), and from there it distributes widely throughout the body, including to injury sites. The peptide is small enough to have good systemic bioavailability from this route.

**💉 Intramuscular injection / into the muscle (used in some protocols)**

Some practitioners prefer IM injection for targeted tissue delivery, particularly for acute muscle injuries. Absorption is similar to or slightly faster than subcutaneous.

### 🏥 Intravenous / IV (clinical research only)

Phase I safety trials used IV administration to test safety at high doses. Not appropriate for self-use. The systemic distribution from subcutaneous is sufficient for therapeutic purposes.

### 💊 Oral / by mouth (not effective)

TB-500 is a peptide chain that would be broken down in the gut before reaching the bloodstream. Oral use is not a valid route.

## Dosage Context

- Lower end of the reported range: 2–2.5 mg subcutaneously, 2–3 times per week (total of 4–5 mg administered per week). This is a conservative starting point to assess tolerance.
- Typical range reported in the literature: 5 mg subcutaneously twice weekly (10 mg/week total) during a 4–6 week loading phase. After noticeable improvement, drop to a maintenance dose of 2.5–5 mg every 2–4 weeks.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: 5 mg twice weekly for acute, severe injuries or during an intensive 4–6 week loading phase. Some sources reference up to 10–15 mg administered per week in short loading phases, but the standard clinical practice keeps it at 5 mg per injection as the upper practical limit.

## Timing & Cycling

There is no strict time-of-day requirement for TB-500. For athletic use, some practitioners time injections close to training sessions — either before or after — theorizing this aligns delivery with tissue repair signals. For injury recovery, starting as soon as possible after the injury (within the first week) may improve outcomes. Daily timing is flexible; consistency of injection days matters more. There are no food restrictions.

TB-500 is cycled. A standard protocol is a 4–6 week loading phase (more frequent dosing) followed by a maintenance phase (monthly or bi-weekly injections), with the overall cycle lasting 8–12 weeks. After the cycle, a rest period of several weeks to months is typical before repeating. Long-term continuous daily use is not recommended. The cycling approach prevents any habituation and allows assessment of whether the underlying injury or condition has resolved. For ongoing joint or tendon issues, some practitioners use a low-dose maintenance protocol indefinitely, but this should be under medical supervision.

Important: TB-500 (TB500) is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
