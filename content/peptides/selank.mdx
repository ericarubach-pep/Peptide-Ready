## What It Is

Selank — a 7-amino-acid synthetic peptide (an extended lab-made version of tuftsin, a natural immune-regulating fragment found in certain blood proteins)

## What It Does

Selank acts as a natural-feeling anxiety reducer by gently tuning the brain's calming chemical system (GABA, the same system targeted by anti-anxiety drugs like Valium) — but without causing sedation, memory problems, or addiction. It also boosts levels of a brain growth factor (BDNF) in the memory center of the brain, supports a balanced mood by modulating serotonin and dopamine, and slows the breakdown of the brain's own natural feel-good peptides.

## Potential Benefits

- May significantly reduce anxiety — clinical studies show effectiveness comparable to standard anti-anxiety medications but without sedation or dependence risk
- Could improve mood and reduce the effects of chronic stress
- Research suggests cognitive benefits including sharper memory and better mental focus
- May improve sleep quality without causing drowsiness
- Could have immune-supporting effects inherited from the natural peptide it's based on
- Currently registered as a pharmaceutical drug in Russia and Ukraine for anxiety disorders

## Where It Came From

Designed and synthesized in Moscow, Russia, at the Institute of Molecular Genetics and the V.V. Zakusov Research Institute of Pharmacology in the 1990s, primarily by researchers N.F. Myasoedov and S.B. Seredenin.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

**👃 Nasal spray / intranasal (most effective and most studied route for brain effects)**

Selank was developed and approved in Russia specifically as a nasal spray. The nasal passage provides a direct pathway to the brain via the olfactory nerve (the nerve responsible for smell), allowing the peptide to reach the central nervous system more directly and rapidly than injection. Clinical Russian studies used intranasal administration. Effects on anxiety and cognition can be felt within minutes.

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (also effective for systemic delivery)**

Subcutaneous injection provides reliable absorption into the bloodstream and is used by biohackers and clinics seeking systemic immune effects or when nasal spray is not available. Slightly slower onset for brain effects compared to nasal spray.

### 💊 Oral / by mouth (very low effectiveness)

Selank as a peptide would be substantially broken down by digestion. No validated oral protocols exist.

## Dosage Context

- Lower end of the reported range: 100–250 mcg administered per day (nasal spray or subcutaneous). For nasal spray at standard Russian pharmacy concentrations (0.15% solution = 150 mcg per 100 mcL spray), this is typically 1–2 sprays per nostril.
- Typical range reported in the literature: 250–500 mcg administered per day, divided into 1–3 administrations. Research studies frequently used 300–500 mcg administered per day. For anxiety or cognitive support, doses are often split: morning and early afternoon.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: 500–900 mcg administered per day. Above 900 mcg administered per day is outside common protocol ranges and not supported by clinical data. Body weight does not typically change the dose, though individual sensitivity varies significantly.

## Timing & Cycling

Morning and/or early-afternoon dosing is typical for Selank. It has calming but also subtly focus-enhancing effects, so evening doses may interfere with sleep for some users — especially intranasal forms, which act quickly. Late-afternoon or evening doses are generally avoided unless personal response has already been established. No food timing requirement, though many users take it on an empty stomach in the morning for cleaner onset. Some users dose it as-needed before stressful events rather than on a fixed daily schedule.

Selank is most commonly used in defined cycles of 10–20 days, followed by a break of similar length. This mirrors Russian clinical protocols. Some users do longer cycles of 4–6 weeks followed by 1–2 weeks off. Unlike benzodiazepines, Selank is not associated with physical dependence, and there is no evidence that it causes withdrawal. However, cycling is recommended to prevent the brain adapting to the modulated GABA and serotonin signaling and losing responsiveness. Some users take it daily for weeks at a time without issues, but periodic breaks are generally advised to maintain effectiveness over the long term.

Important: Selank is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
