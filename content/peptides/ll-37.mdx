## What It Is

LL-37 — a 37-amino-acid peptide and the only member of the cathelicidin family found in humans (a natural antibiotic your immune cells produce to fight infection)

## What It Does

LL-37 is part of your body's first line of defense. It works like a biological detergent: it's attracted to the outer surface of bacteria (which has a negative electrical charge), locks on, and punches holes in the bacterial membrane, causing the cell to fall apart. Beyond killing microbes directly, it also acts as a distress flare — recruiting immune cells to the infection site, promoting new blood vessel growth for wound healing, and triggering your cells' internal trash-disposal system to eliminate bacteria hiding inside them.

## Potential Benefits

- Directly kills a broad range of bacteria, including drug-resistant strains like MRSA
- May also be effective against certain fungi and viruses with a protective outer envelope
- Could accelerate wound healing and tissue repair
- Research suggests it can help eliminate bacteria hiding inside immune cells, including tuberculosis bacteria
- May have anti-cancer properties, though the evidence is mixed depending on cancer type
- Plays a key role in skin barrier defense — naturally produced by skin and immune cells

## Where It Came From

First identified and characterized in 1995 by Gudmundsson and colleagues at the Karolinska Institute in Sweden, while studying the family of antimicrobial proteins produced by immune cells.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (preferred for systemic antimicrobial and immune effects)**

Direct delivery into the bloodstream ensures the highest bioavailability and is the most studied route for systemic benefits. The peptide's short half-life means subcutaneous injection is preferred over slower-absorbing routes.

**🧴 Topical / applied to skin or wound (highly effective for localized skin infections and wound healing)**

Applied directly to the site of infection or wound as a gel or cream, delivering LL-37 exactly where the antimicrobial action is needed. Common concentrations range from 0.1–1% in formulations. Excellent for skin infections, rosacea, and wound care.

### 👃 Intranasal (spray

used for respiratory/sinus infections) — The nasal lining and airways are natural sites where LL-37 is produced by the body, making nasal spray a logical delivery route for respiratory and sinus applications. Some protocols use 0.5–1 mg per spray, 1–2 times daily.

**💊 Oral / by mouth (not established for systemic use; may have gut-specific effects)**

LL-37 is not currently supported by evidence for oral administration for systemic goals. Some research explores its effects on the gut microbiome when administered orally, but this is not a standard protocol.

## Dosage Context

- Lower end of the reported range: 100–200 mcg administered per day subcutaneously, once daily, 5 days per week. Starting at the low end is strongly advised because LL-37 can cause immune overstimulation and injection site irritation at higher doses in new users.
- Typical range reported in the literature: 0.5–1 mg (500–1,000 mcg) per injection, given 2–3 times per week subcutaneously. This is the range most commonly referenced in clinical and research-adjacent protocols.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: Up to 2 mg per injection, 2–3 times per week, reserved for specific therapeutic applications (severe infections, wound healing) under physician supervision. Advanced protocols of up to 500 mcg administered per day are mentioned in some sources, but going above 1 mg per injection without medical oversight is not recommended. Body weight does not typically change the dose in current protocols.

## Timing & Cycling

For infection or immune support, daily or every-other-day injections in the morning are the most common approach. LL-37 does not require fasting or food timing in the same way that GH-axis peptides do. For wound healing or topical use, apply directly to the wound bed or skin at least once or twice daily. The peptide's relatively short half-life means more frequent dosing (daily or every other day rather than once weekly) maintains more consistent therapeutic levels.

LL-37 is used in short, targeted cycles rather than continuously. Standard cycles for acute conditions run 2–4 weeks, followed by at least 2 weeks off. For longer-term immune support or chronic conditions, cycles of 4–8 weeks (acute) to 8–12 weeks (chronic) are described, with breaks between. Short cycling is partly due to LL-37's strong immune-stimulating nature — prolonged continuous use could potentially cause excessive immune activation. It is not a drug meant for indefinite daily use. After completing a course, reassessment of the underlying condition determines whether another cycle is needed.

Important: LL-37 is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
