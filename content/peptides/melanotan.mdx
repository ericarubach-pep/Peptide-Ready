## What It Is

Melanotan-I (also known as Afamelanotide) and Melanotan-II — lab-made peptides modeled after a natural skin-signaling hormone (alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone) that tells skin cells to produce pigment

## What It Does

Both Melanotan versions work by triggering sensors on your skin cells (called melanocortin receptors) that tell them to ramp up production of the dark, UV-protective pigment called eumelanin — essentially giving you a tan without sun exposure. Melanotan-I (Afamelanotide) mainly sticks to the skin-pigment receptor, while Melanotan-II activates a wider set of receptors, including ones in the brain that control sexual arousal and appetite. Melanotan-I also activates DNA repair pathways in skin cells that help prevent sun-damage mutations.

## Potential Benefits

- Melanotan-I is FDA- and EU-approved for a rare disorder (erythropoietic protoporphyria) that causes severe pain from sun exposure — it significantly increased pain-free sun time in clinical trials
- May provide UV protection by boosting the skin's natural pigment barrier
- Research suggests Melanotan-I could help prevent sun-related DNA damage in skin
- Melanotan-II may help treat sexual dysfunction (its derivative PT-141/bremelanotide is FDA-approved for low sexual desire in women)
- Melanotan-II may suppress appetite
- Important note: Melanotan-II is NOT approved for medical use and carries serious safety concerns, including possible links to skin cancer and other severe side effects

## Where It Came From

Developed by Victor Hruby, Mac Hadley, and colleagues at the University of Arizona, USA, starting in the 1980s; Melanotan-I was first synthesized in 1984 and Melanotan-II in 1991, with the first human trials published in 1996.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (most effective, standard route)**

Almost all research and practical protocols use subcutaneous injection into the fatty tissue around the belly button area. This ensures direct systemic delivery and reliable pigmentation effects. Both MT-1 (afamelanotide) and MT-2 are used this way.

**👃 Nasal spray (commonly used in practice, lower effectiveness per mg)**

A very common self-administration method because it avoids needles. Nasal sprays do allow partial absorption through the nasal lining, but bioavailability is significantly lower than subcutaneous injection — meaning more peptide is needed for the same effect, and results are more variable.

### 💊 Oral / by mouth (not effective)

Melanotan peptides are broken down by stomach acid and digestive enzymes and do not produce meaningful systemic effects when swallowed. There are no validated oral protocols.

### 🏥 IV (not used in standard protocols)

Not a practical route for either MT-1 or MT-2 in clinical or self-use settings.

Note: MT-1 (afamelanotide) is FDA-approved in the U.S. as Scenesse for erythropoietic protoporphyria (a rare skin disease). MT-2 is not approved anywhere and is sold as a "research chemical." Both carry serious safety concerns including increased risk of new or changing moles and potential melanoma risk.

## Dosage Context

- Lower end of the loading-phase range: 0.25 mg (250 mcg) per day subcutaneously during the loading phase. Starting at the very low end minimizes nausea and facial flushing, which are common side effects.
- Typical loading-phase range: 0.5–1 mg (500–1,000 mcg) per day subcutaneously for 7–14 days until the desired skin tone is reached. Some protocols use 0.01 mg/kg (10 mcg/kg) as a body-weight-based starting point.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: Up to 1 mg administered per day during loading is described as an upper bound, with protocols generally not going beyond this during the loading phase. Maintenance doses are much lower: 0.5–1 mg 1–2 times per week after the desired color is achieved, primarily to maintain tone. The FDA-approved MT-1 implant (Scenesse) delivers 16 mg every 2 months via subcutaneous implant, but this is under strict medical supervision.

## Timing & Cycling

Administer via injection in the evening, ideally 30–60 minutes before bed, to minimize the window of nausea and facial flushing (which peak in the 1–2 hours after injection). UV exposure (sunlight or tanning bed) 2–3 times per week during the loading phase significantly amplifies the pigmentation effect — the peptide stimulates melanocytes but sun exposure activates them. Without some UV exposure, tanning results are reduced. Maintenance injections are done on a flexible schedule — 1–2 times per week as needed to sustain color.

Melanotan protocols naturally follow a two-phase structure: a loading phase (daily injections for 1–2 weeks) and a maintenance phase (1–2 injections per week indefinitely, or seasonally). Many users cycle it seasonally — loading in spring/summer and stopping in fall/winter. There is no established off-cycle requirement for safety purposes, but given the serious concerns about mole changes and potential cancer risk, prolonged continuous use at high doses is not recommended. MT-2 specifically is associated with significant sexual side effects (spontaneous erections in men) and is not intended for long-term daily use.

Important: Melanotan (MT-1 / MT-2) is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
