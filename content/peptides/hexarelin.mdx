## What It Is

Hexarelin — a 6-amino-acid lab-made peptide (a synthetic relative of ghrelin, the 'hunger hormone,' engineered to powerfully trigger growth hormone release)

## What It Does

Hexarelin works by activating two different docking sites in your body — one in the brain and pituitary gland (the growth hormone control center) and one directly in the heart. Activating the first site causes a strong surge of growth hormone. Activating the second site protects the heart from damage caused by reduced blood flow, independent of growth hormone entirely. This dual action makes it notable for both muscle and metabolism support and cardiovascular protection.

## Potential Benefits

- Potently stimulates growth hormone release, peaking around 30 minutes after use
- May protect the heart against damage from reduced blood flow (like during a heart attack)
- In human clinical studies, significantly improved how strongly the heart pumps blood
- Could support muscle preservation and reduce age-related muscle loss
- Research suggests potential for treating growth hormone deficiency and heart failure
- May help improve overall cardiac output and circulation

## Where It Came From

Developed by Romano Deghenghi and colleagues at Europeptides in France/Italy in the early 1990s as a more potent version of an existing growth hormone-releasing peptide; the first human study was published in 1994.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

### 💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (standard, most used)

Used in virtually all human and animal research. Delivers the peptide efficiently into the bloodstream without digestive breakdown. This is the go-to route for both the growth-hormone-releasing effects and the cardioprotective effects Hexarelin is studied for.

### 💉 Intravenous (IV) injection (used in research, faster onset)

Research studies have administered Hexarelin IV to study rapid GH responses. Produces the fastest and sharpest GH pulse but is not practical for routine self-administration.

### 💊 Oral / by mouth (very low effectiveness)

Studies confirm that Hexarelin can cause GH release when taken orally, but the dose required is dramatically higher than injectable forms because digestion destroys most of the peptide. Oral use is considered impractical for consistent results.

### 👃 Nasal spray (limited evidence)

Some compounding pharmacies offer nasal formulations. Absorption through the nasal lining can bypass some digestive breakdown, but there are far fewer studies on this route compared to injection for Hexarelin.

## Dosage Context

- Lower end of the reported range: 100 mcg per injection, subcutaneously, once daily (typically before bed). Some weight-based starting points: 100 mcg for individuals under 90 kg (roughly 198 lbs).
- Typical range reported in the literature: 100–200 mcg per injection. Most protocols administer via injection once daily at bedtime. Some intermediate protocols use 200 mcg once or twice daily.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: 200 mcg once or twice daily. One well-cited clinical protocol uses 0.2 mg (200 mcg) subcutaneously every night for the first month, then every other night. Doses much above 200 mcg per injection are not commonly used because Hexarelin is known to cause receptor desensitization at high or continuous doses, reducing its effectiveness over time. Body weight can factor in: 100 mcg for under 90 kg, 150 mcg for 90–110 kg, 200 mcg for over 110 kg.

## Timing & Cycling

The standard approach described in the literature is bedtime dosing of Hexarelin. Growth hormone is naturally released in pulses during deep sleep; dosing a GHRP (growth hormone releasing peptide) like Hexarelin in the evening is described as amplifying this natural overnight pulse. Injecting on an empty stomach (2+ hours after the last meal) is strongly recommended, since high insulin from a recent meal blunts GH release — eating before a GHRP injection significantly reduces its effectiveness. Carbohydrate-heavy meals in the hours before the evening dose are generally avoided for best reported results.

Hexarelin is well-documented to cause receptor desensitization with continuous daily use — meaning the body becomes less responsive over time. For this reason, it is almost always cycled. A typical protocol runs 12–16 weeks, followed by a mandatory 4-week break. Some protocols switch to every-other-night dosing after the first month to slow the development of desensitization. Many practitioners recommend cycling Hexarelin in combination with a GHRH (growth hormone releasing hormone) peptide like CJC-1295, then alternating. Unlike GLP-1 drugs (e.g., Ozempic), which are designed for continuous long-term use, Hexarelin is not intended to be taken indefinitely without breaks.

Important: Hexarelin is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
