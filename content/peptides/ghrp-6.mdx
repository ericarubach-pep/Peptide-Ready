## What It Is

GHRP-6 (Growth Hormone-Releasing Peptide-6) — a 6-amino-acid synthetic peptide and the original member of the growth hormone-releasing peptide family, first developed in the early 1980s. It mimics ghrelin to stimulate growth hormone release.

## What It Does

GHRP-6 was the first synthetic molecule ever shown to specifically trigger a dose-dependent burst of growth hormone from the pituitary gland. Like GHRP-2, it activates both the ghrelin receptor (which triggers growth hormone release) and a second receptor called CD36 found in the heart. CD36 activation sets off a chain reaction that protects heart cells from dying during a heart attack, reduces harmful inflammation, and cuts down on scarring in the heart and liver. It also stimulates appetite noticeably — and unlike ipamorelin, it mildly raises cortisol too. The growth hormone boost it produces is actually blunted if you take it after a carb- or fat-heavy meal.

## Potential Benefits

- Triggers reliable, dose-dependent growth hormone release for body composition and anti-aging use
- Strong animal evidence for heart protection — one study showed it rescued over 70% of heart tissue at risk of dying during a heart attack
- May reduce liver inflammation and scarring in preclinical studies
- Could improve memory, muscle growth, and metabolic health based on animal research
- Anti-scarring properties demonstrated in liver and skin scar models
- Has been administered intravenously in healthy human volunteers without significant safety problems

## Where It Came From

Discovered by Cyril Y. Bowers at Tulane University (New Orleans, USA) in the early 1980s, making it the founding member of the entire growth hormone-releasing peptide family. Bowers observed that chemical relatives of a natural pain-modulating compound (met-enkephalin) could stimulate growth hormone release from pituitary cells. His discovery of this peptide class ultimately inspired Japanese researchers Kangawa and colleagues to find ghrelin — the natural hormone that uses the same receptor — in 1999.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (standard and most effective)**

The primary route for GHRP-6 in research and practice. GH release begins within 5–15 minutes of injection; the hunger-stimulating effect (a key feature of GHRP-6) kicks in within 15–30 minutes. Standard abdominal or thigh injection.

### 🏥 Intravenous / IV (most potent, research/clinical only)

Like GHRP-2, IV gives the highest and fastest GH pulse. Used in research comparisons but not for practical self-use.

### 👃 Intranasal / nasal spray (low-moderate)

GHRP-6 has some activity via nasal delivery, but bioavailability is much lower than injection.

### 💊 Oral / by mouth (studied, some limited activity)

Unlike most peptides, GHRP-6 has been shown to retain some GH-releasing activity orally (Bowers et al., 1995), but potency is far lower than subcutaneous injection. Oral GHRP-6 is not a practical primary route.

## Dosage Context

- GHRP-6 is similar in dosing to GHRP-2 but is notable for causing significant hunger (ghrelin mimicry), which can be a benefit (muscle building) or drawback (fat loss goals):
- Lower end of the reported range: 100 mcg subcutaneously once daily at bedtime, for the first week (to gauge the hunger response before increasing frequency).
- Typical range reported in the literature: 100 mcg per injection, 2–3 times daily (morning, pre-workout, and/or bedtime), for 8–16 weeks. Three daily injections produce pulsatile GH release that mimics natural secretion patterns.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: Up to 200–300 mcg per injection, 2–3 times daily. Like GHRP-2, the GH response largely saturates around 100–200 mcg per injection; higher doses mostly increase hunger without proportionally more GH.
- Note: GHRP-6 is generally considered less potent per mcg than GHRP-2 for pure GH output, but causes more appetite stimulation.

## Timing & Cycling

Same rules as GHRP-2 are typically followed: dosing on an empty stomach (at least 2 hours after eating), with a 20–30 minute wait before eating afterward. This is critical because fats and carbohydrates blunt the GH response. Best times: morning fasted, pre-workout, and before bed. Significant hunger (a ghrelin-like effect) within 15–30 minutes of dosing is commonly reported — meal timing is often planned around this, especially when the goal is fat loss and avoiding overeating. For muscle gain, the hunger response can be used strategically to time a post-injection meal.

Same cycling rationale as GHRP-2. GHRP-6 can cause receptor desensitization (tachyphylaxis) with continuous use, and response may diminish over time. Standard cycle: 8–16 weeks on, followed by 4–8 weeks off. A 5-days-on / 2-days-off weekly schedule within a cycle is commonly used. The 'off' period allows the GHSR receptor to recover sensitivity. Often stacked and co-cycled with a GHRH analog (such as CJC-1295 or Mod GRF 1-29) for synergistic GH amplification.

Important: GHRP-6 is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
