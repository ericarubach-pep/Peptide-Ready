## What It Is

Retatrutide — a 39-amino-acid synthetic peptide that simultaneously activates three different hormone receptors in your body — GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon — making it the first "triple agonist" weight-loss drug. Developed by Eli Lilly (code name LY3437943).

## What It Does

Think of it as a three-key master remote that hits three different hormonal channels at once. The GLP-1 channel (like Ozempic) tells your brain you're full and tells your pancreas to release insulin. The GIP channel amplifies that insulin signal and helps your fat cells release stored fat. The glucagon channel tells your liver to burn fat rather than store it, and ramps up how many calories you burn at rest. Hitting all three pathways simultaneously produces weight loss far beyond what any single- or dual-target drug can achieve.

## Potential Benefits

- Phase 2 trials showed an average of 17–24% body weight loss at 48 weeks — the largest weight loss ever recorded in a phase 2 drug trial
- Nearly 100% of people at the highest dose lost at least 5% of body weight
- Over 90% of participants with fatty liver disease showed normalized liver fat levels
- Significantly reduced blood sugar in people with type 2 diabetes
- Could reduce cardiovascular disease risk given the massive metabolic improvements
- Phase 3 trials (expected 2024–2026) will determine whether it earns FDA approval

## Where It Came From

Developed by Eli Lilly and Company (Indianapolis, USA). Phase 2 results were published in the New England Journal of Medicine and The Lancet in mid-2023, generating significant excitement. It builds on Lilly's earlier drug tirzepatide (Mounjaro) by adding a third receptor target.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

### 💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (only established route)

All clinical trials of Retatrutide have used once-weekly subcutaneous injection. The peptide is given into the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm. This is a GLP-1/GIP/glucagon triple-receptor agonist in the same drug class as Semaglutide and Tirzepatide, all of which are administered this way.

### 💊 Oral / by mouth (not yet available)

No approved oral form exists. Like other large peptides in this class, oral delivery would require extremely high doses due to gut breakdown, and this has not been developed for Retatrutide.

### 💉 IV / intramuscular (not used clinically)

Not part of any established protocol for this drug.

## Dosage Context

- Note: Retatrutide is not yet FDA-approved (as of mid-2026) and all dosing comes from clinical trials (Phase 2 and Phase 3 TRIUMPH program). Expect final approved dosing may differ slightly.
- Lower end of the reported range: 0.5–2 mg once weekly. Some protocols start at 0.5 mg, others at 2 mg — the 2 mg initial range was used in Phase 3 trials because starting at 4 mg caused more GI side effects. The first few weeks are described as being about the body adjusting, not achieving weight loss.
- Typical range reported in the literature: 4–9 mg once weekly, reached after gradual escalation over several months. At this range most people see significant appetite reduction and weight loss.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: 12 mg once weekly — the highest dose studied in trials and associated with the most weight loss (up to ~24–29% body weight at 48 weeks in Phase 2). Escalation to 12 mg typically takes about 16–20 weeks of gradual step-ups. Not everyone needs or tolerates this dose; 8–9 mg is an effective maintenance dose for many.
- Dose escalation steps (typical Phase 3 protocol): 2 mg → 4 mg → 6 mg → 9 mg → 12 mg, increasing every 4 weeks.

## Timing & Cycling

Once weekly, on the same day each week, at any time of day with or without food. Consistent timing (same weekday each week) is important. If a dose is missed within 3 days, the missed dose is typically taken as soon as remembered. If more than 3 days have passed, the missed dose is typically skipped in favor of the next scheduled dose. Double-dosing to catch up is generally avoided.

Retatrutide is designed as a continuous long-term treatment, not a cycling drug. Like Semaglutide and other GLP-1-class drugs, the weight and metabolic benefits are tied to ongoing use — stopping causes appetite to return and weight to come back. The gradual dose escalation phase lasts 4–5 months, after which the patient stays on their maintenance dose indefinitely as long as they benefit and tolerate it. There is no established evidence-based reason to cycle off. Any breaks should be medical decisions (e.g., surgery, side effects), not planned cycling.

Important: Retatrutide is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
