## What It Is

Humanin — a 24-amino-acid peptide produced inside your mitochondria (the energy-generating organelles in your cells), making it unusual because most proteins are made from the main cell nucleus, not the mitochondria

## What It Does

Humanin is a tiny protein your mitochondria release as a distress signal — its levels are highest when cells are healthy and drop as you age or when mitochondria are damaged. Once secreted, it acts like a cellular bodyguard: it blocks multiple pathways that would otherwise cause cells to self-destruct, helping neurons, heart cells, and reproductive cells survive stress, toxic exposure, and age-related wear. It also improves how the body handles fat and blood sugar.

## Potential Benefits

- May protect brain cells from the kind of damage seen in Alzheimer's disease
- Research suggests it reduces body fat and improves lean muscle mass in aging animals
- Could improve the body's ability to manage blood sugar and insulin sensitivity
- May protect the heart against damage from interrupted blood flow
- Research in simple organisms (roundworms) shows it can extend lifespan
- Could protect reproductive cells from chemotherapy-related damage
- May serve as a biological marker for mitochondrial health and overall aging

## Where It Came From

Discovered in 2001 by Yasuhiro Hashimoto and colleagues at the Tokyo Institute of Psychiatry and Keio University in Japan, while searching for proteins that could protect brain cells from the toxic plaques associated with Alzheimer's disease.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

### 💉 Injection into the abdominal cavity / intraperitoneal (IP)

Used exclusively in animal studies; not a human route. Mentioned only for research context.

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (most practical for human use, extrapolated from animal research)**

The majority of animal research shows meaningful effects with systemic injection. Subcutaneous injection is the most commonly discussed route in emerging human protocols and longevity circles, as it delivers the peptide into the bloodstream efficiently.

### 👃 Intranasal (nasal spray

limited but promising data for brain targeting) — Research with related analogs (e.g., Colivelin, which includes Humanin) suggests intranasal delivery can reach brain tissue more directly than systemic injection, bypassing the blood-brain barrier. This may be relevant for neuroprotection goals but is not yet well-established for standard Humanin itself.

### 💊 Oral / by mouth (not established, likely very low effectiveness)

No peer-reviewed data supports oral bioavailability for Humanin. As a 24-amino acid peptide, it would be expected to be largely broken down by digestion.

IMPORTANT: Humanin has not been tested in clinical human trials. All dosing and route information is extrapolated from animal research. Therapeutic dosing in humans has not been established.

## Dosage Context

- Critical note: No human clinical dosing has been established for Humanin. All figures below are extrapolated from animal studies and should be understood as research reference points, not clinical recommendations.
- Animal study reference (extrapolated): The potent analog HNG was used at 0.1 mcg injected every other day for 3 months in mice. A more common animal dose for the standard analog HNG is 50–100 mcg/kg intraperitoneally. Translating 50 mcg/kg to a 70 kg human gives a rough reference of ~3,500 mcg (3.5 mg), though direct species-to-species extrapolation is unreliable.
- Emerging human protocol reference (Paragons Sports Medicine / longevity clinics): Some compounding clinics list Humanin protocols but emphasize physician supervision and individualized dosing. No standardized beginner/advanced tiers exist.
- In short: there is no validated beginner, average, or Higher end of the range documented in protocols for Humanin in humans at this time.

## Timing & Cycling

Because there are no validated human protocols, timing recommendations are largely theoretical. In mouse studies, injections were administered every other day without a specific time-of-day requirement. If used under medical supervision, morning injection is a common default for systemic peptides with metabolic or neuroprotective goals, though no circadian rationale has been established specifically for Humanin.

No established cycling protocol exists for Humanin in humans. In animal studies, continuous every-other-day administration for 3-month courses was used. Biohacking-adjacent communities and some longevity clinics suggest following patterns similar to other mitochondrial peptides — short courses of several weeks followed by a break — but this is not evidence-based for Humanin specifically. Given the complete absence of human clinical trial data, anyone exploring Humanin should do so only under close medical supervision with individualized protocols.

Important: Humanin is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
