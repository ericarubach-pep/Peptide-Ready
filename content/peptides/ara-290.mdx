## What It Is

ARA 290 (cibinetide) — an 11-amino-acid synthetic peptide derived from a specific surface region of erythropoietin (EPO, the hormone that stimulates red blood cell production), engineered to capture EPO's tissue-repair powers without triggering blood cell production.

## What It Does

EPO is known as the "blood doping" hormone because it stimulates the body to make more red blood cells — but scientists discovered it also has a completely separate, powerful tissue-repair function. ARA 290 is a tiny fragment from EPO's surface that activates only the repair pathway (through a receptor complex called the "innate repair receptor") without touching the blood-cell production pathway. Once it locks onto this repair receptor — found on pain-sensing nerve fibers, immune cells, and kidney cells — it shuts down pain signaling, damps down inflammation, and actually stimulates damaged nerve fibers to physically regrow.

## Potential Benefits

- Phase 2 clinical trials showed significant improvement in nerve pain scores in patients with sarcoidosis (a chronic inflammatory disease)
- Research showed measurable regrowth of nerve fibers in the cornea of the eye — a rare demonstration of physical nerve regeneration in humans
- May improve pain tolerance and physical functioning in neuropathy patients
- Could improve blood sugar control and nerve pain in type 2 diabetes based on Phase 2 data
- Does not affect red blood cell production — confirmed safe in human trials
- Potential future applications in kidney disease and other conditions involving damaged nerve fibers

## Where It Came From

Emerged from research by Michael Brines and Anthony Cerami at Araim Pharmaceuticals and The Feinstein Institute in the USA. The key insight — that EPO's repair function is completely separate from its blood cell-making function — came from a landmark 2004 paper, and the specific 11-amino-acid fragment was identified and characterized in a 2008 publication.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (standard clinical and research route)**

All Phase II clinical trials used subcutaneous self-injection. ARA 290 has high bioavailability (greater than 85%) by this route and is easily self-administered. This is the only route with direct human safety and efficacy data.

### 🏥 Intravenous / IV (used in early research only)

A 2012 pilot trial used IV administration 3 times per week. This route delivers 100% of the dose but requires clinical access and is not practical for regular use.

### 💊 Oral / by mouth (not studied or established)

No oral form or oral bioavailability data exists for ARA 290. It should be assumed that oral use would not be effective.

## Dosage Context

- ARA 290 has unusually clean dosing data from controlled clinical trials:
- Conservative/Lower end of the reported range: 2 mg subcutaneously once daily for 28 days. Used in sarcoidosis trials as the lower-range dose; shows efficacy with a conservative safety margin.
- Standard/Typical range reported in the literature: 4 mg subcutaneously once daily for 28 days. This is the most-validated dose from Phase II trials in diabetic neuropathy and sarcoidosis-associated nerve pain. No additional benefit was seen at 8 mg, so 4 mg is considered the therapeutic ceiling.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: 8 mg administered daily was tested and found to offer no advantage over 4 mg. There is no established clinical rationale for going above 4 mg/day.
- Alternative protocol: Weight-based dosing of approximately 0.03 mg/kg every 2 days for the first 2 weeks, then weekly for weeks 3–15 (the longest validated protocol at 15 weeks). For an 80 kg person, that is about 2.4 mg per dose.
- Dose does not appear to need body weight adjustment for the standard 4 mg protocol.

## Timing & Cycling

Administer via injection once daily subcutaneously at any consistent time — morning, afternoon, or evening. There is no evidence that time of day matters significantly for ARA 290's mechanism (tissue protection and nerve repair signaling). Rotating injection sites between the thigh, abdomen, and upper arm prevents local irritation. Consistent daily timing is more important than specific hour.

ARA 290 is used in cycles, not continuously long-term. The best-validated cycle is 28 days of daily injections, followed by reassessment. Clinical trial data showed effects that persisted for at least one month after stopping the 28-day protocol, suggesting the peptide's benefits continue beyond the active treatment period. The longest studied protocol is 15 weeks using a tapering schedule. There is no established evidence for continuous year-round use, and the current medical community view is to use defined cycles with monitoring between them. Development of this compound stalled when Araim Pharmaceuticals shut down, so protocols remain based on Phase II data only.

Important: ARA 290 is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
