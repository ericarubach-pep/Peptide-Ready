## What It Is

Epithalon (also spelled Epitalon) — a synthetic tetrapeptide (4 amino acids, Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly) modeled on Epithalamin, a natural peptide extract from the pineal gland

## What It Does

Think of your pineal gland as the body's built-in conductor, keeping the daily sleep-wake clock and long-term aging clock in sync. Epithalon is researched for its ability to reach into that timing system and nudge it back toward a more youthful pattern. Its signature effect is reactivating telomerase — the enzyme that rebuilds the protective caps (telomeres) on the ends of your chromosomes, similar to re-capping the frayed tip of a shoelace so it stops unraveling. In lab studies, this reactivation let treated human cells keep dividing well past the point where they would normally stop (known as the Hayflick limit). Alongside its telomere effects, Epithalon has also been shown to help restore melatonin production and circadian "clock gene" signaling, and has demonstrated antioxidant, immune-modulating, and neuroprotective activity in animal studies.

## Potential Benefits

- Cell culture studies show Epithalon activates telomerase and lengthens telomeres in normal human cells, allowing them to divide well beyond the standard Hayflick limit
- A 2025 independent study from Brunel University London confirmed telomerase upregulation in human fibroblast and breast epithelial cell lines — the first major Western replication of the original Russian findings
- Animal studies suggest modest lifespan extension (roughly 12–24% increases in maximum lifespan in some rodent cohorts), alongside reduced chromosomal abnormalities and lower tumor incidence
- Research points to restored melatonin production and improved circadian "clock gene" regulation, which may support more consistent sleep patterns
- Nasal-delivery studies in animals suggest neuroprotective and cognitive benefits, including reversing some age-related brain changes and increasing cortical neuron activity
- Long-term observational data on coronary patients using the parent compound (Epithalamin) suggests lower cardiovascular and all-cause mortality, though this is not a randomized controlled trial

## Where It Came From

Epithalon traces back to Soviet-era military medical research in Leningrad (now Saint Petersburg) in the 1970s, where physician-scientist Vladimir Khavinson and colleague Vladimir Morozov studied whether short peptides isolated from animal organs could restore function in aging tissue — a concept they called "bioregulation." Their starting point was Epithalamin, a crude polypeptide extract from bovine pineal glands; Khavinson's team at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology later identified and synthesized its active four-amino-acid fragment, naming it Epithalon. The defining study came in 2003, when Khavinson's group reported that Epithalon reactivated telomerase and elongated telomeres in telomerase-negative human fetal fibroblasts — a finding that went without independent Western replication for more than two decades, until a 2025 study from Brunel University London confirmed the telomerase-activating effect in separate human cell lines.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

### 💉 Injection (Subcutaneous or Intramuscular) — Clear Gold Standard

Research into Epithalon has primarily focused on two routes: intranasal and injection. The main reason for choosing subcutaneous Epithalon over a nasal spray is bioavailability — animal and human studies alike have demonstrated the high absorption and bioavailability of Epithalon when injected, and subcutaneous administration is the most common means of delivering the vast majority of peptides. Based on available scientific studies, Epithalon was typically administered at a dose of 10 mg injected subcutaneously three times a week for three weeks, with this cycle repeated once a year. According to another method, 10 mg is injected intramuscularly every day for 10 consecutive days annually. The main drawbacks are the need to reconstitute the powder with bacteriostatic water and occasional mild injection site reactions.

### 👃 Nasal Spray — Second Best, Useful for Neuro/Brain Goals

In rats, administration of a nasal spray preparation of Epithalon was shown to increase cortical neuron activity. Peptide nasal sprays are considered to be much more "patient-friendly" compared to repeated subcutaneous injections, though the principal challenge is limited bioavailability. Epithalon nasal spray has exhibited notable anti-aging properties particularly concerning brain aging, with research demonstrating the peptide's capability to reverse age-related alterations in aging mice, and showing potential in mitigating cognitive decline and enhancing neuroplasticity. An interesting route is through the nasal mucosa in a saline solution with 3% DMSO, which is reported to greatly increase absorption of the peptide. Research on nasal Epithalon is limited, but some studies have suggested it may influence circadian rhythm and melatonin production.

### 💊 Oral Capsules — Least Effective

Oral Epithalon is the least effective delivery method due to enzymatic breakdown in the digestive tract, which limits its bioavailability, and there is little evidence to support clinical benefits from oral dosing. Oral and nasal spray versions have significantly lower bioavailability (estimated 5–15%) compared to injection, meaning you would need much higher doses to achieve similar effects, making these methods less cost-effective and less reliable. Some experimental formulations use sublingual drops to bypass digestion, but data remains minimal and largely anecdotal — one human trial used 0.5 mg/day sublingually for 20 days and reported changes in melatonin metabolite excretion and clock-gene expression.

## Dosage Context

- Lower end of the reported range: 2–5 mg administered per day via subcutaneous injection for a 10-day course. This lower range is described as allowing tolerance to be assessed before committing to a full protocol.
- Typical range reported in the literature: 5–10 mg administered per day via subcutaneous injection for 10–20 consecutive days — the protocol most frequently cited in the Khavinson-led research and in current practitioner use.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: 10 mg administered per day for the full 20-day course sits at the upper end of published research protocols. Some sources report doses up to 50 mg/day tested without stronger results, so going beyond 10 mg/day does not appear to add meaningful benefit.
- Non-injectable routes: Because bioavailability is estimated at just 5–15% for nasal and oral delivery, those routes would need much higher doses to approximate injected effects, and there is little data validating any specific equivalent nasal or oral dose.
- Note: No dose-ranging human trial has directly compared doses head-to-head. All figures are derived from Russian clinical studies (mainly in elderly patients), not formal pharmacokinetic dose-finding work.

## Timing & Cycling

Frequency: Daily dosing is standard during an active cycle — either once-daily subcutaneous injections for 10 to 20 consecutive days, or an intramuscular protocol of once daily for 10 straight days.

Morning vs. night: Evening dosing (roughly 4–6 PM, or 30–60 minutes before bed) is the most commonly recommended timing, since Epithalon is thought to act through pineal gland and melatonin-related pathways that are most active overnight.

Cycle timing across the year: Most protocols run just 1 to 3 short courses per year, spaced roughly 4 to 6 months apart, rather than any ongoing daily or weekly maintenance dosing.

Nasal spray timing: Less established than the injectable protocol, though since its main research use is for cognitive and circadian applications, evening dosing is also commonly suggested to align with sleep-related effects.

Epithalon is one of the more clearly "cycle-only" peptides — it is never intended for continuous, year-round use. The standard published protocol is 10 to 20 consecutive days of daily dosing, repeated just 1 to 3 times per year with a gap of 4 to 6 months between courses.

Why cycle? Khavinson's bioregulator theory holds that these short peptides act as a brief "reset" signal rather than a maintenance therapy — pineal and telomerase pathways are nudged toward a more youthful state during the short course, with effects proposed to persist over the following months without continuous stimulation. Some published data also suggests the telomere-lengthening benefit plateaus after a course, so more frequent dosing does not appear to add meaningful additional benefit.

Important: Epithalon is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
