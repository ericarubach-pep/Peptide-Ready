## What It Is

GHK-Cu (Glycyl-L-Histidyl-L-Lysine Copper Complex) — a 3-amino-acid peptide naturally found in human blood that carries a copper ion (like a tiny delivery vehicle for a mineral your body needs)

## What It Does

GHK-Cu is a small peptide your body already makes — it's most abundant in your blood when you're young and drops significantly as you age. It works by ferrying copper into cells, where that mineral is needed to run over a dozen critical processes, from building connective tissue to fighting oxidative damage. It also acts like a master switch for gene activity, capable of turning more than 4,000 genes up or down — essentially nudging your cells back toward a younger, healthier operating state.

## Potential Benefits

- May speed up wound healing in skin, hair follicles, and the gut lining
- Could stimulate collagen production, helping skin stay firmer and more elastic
- Research suggests it has strong anti-inflammatory effects, reducing key inflammatory signals
- May help reverse damage to skin cells caused by radiation or aging
- Could support brain health and protect against age-related cognitive decline
- In controlled trials, showed increased collagen in 70% of women who used it topically

## Where It Came From

Discovered in 1973 by Loren Pickart at the University of California San Francisco, while studying why older liver tissue started behaving like younger tissue when exposed to a certain protein in human blood.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

**🧴 Topical / applied to skin (most effective for skin and hair goals)**

GHK-Cu was originally found in human blood and skin, and its small size allows it to penetrate the outer layer of skin effectively. For anti-aging skin care, wound healing, or hair follicle stimulation, topical application delivers the peptide directly to the target tissue with minimal waste. This is the most commonly used and best-studied route for cosmetic purposes.

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (most effective for whole-body / systemic effects)**

When systemic benefits are the goal — such as lung tissue support, anti-inflammatory effects, or broader gene-expression changes — subcutaneous injection delivers GHK-Cu into the bloodstream efficiently. Typically dosed at 200–500 mcg per injection.

**💉 Injection into muscle / intramuscular (similar to subcutaneous, slightly different absorption curve)**

Also used for systemic effects; some clinics prefer IM for slightly faster peak absorption, though the practical difference is minimal for most users.

### 💊 Oral / by mouth (least effective)

GHK-Cu is a tripeptide (only 3 amino acids) and may survive digestion better than larger peptides, but stomach acid and digestive enzymes still break down a meaningful portion. Oral forms exist but are considered significantly less reliable than injection or topical for any meaningful systemic effect.

## Dosage Context

- Lower end of the reported range (injectable, systemic): 200–300 mcg per injection, once daily. For topical use, any product containing 1–5% GHK-Cu concentration applied once daily is a standard starting point.
- Typical range reported in the literature (injectable): 300–500 mcg administered per day subcutaneously, often split into two doses of 150–250 mcg. Topical products in clinical settings often use concentrations around 2–5%.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols (injectable): Up to 500–1,000 mcg administered per day for specific indications like wound healing or lung support, under clinical supervision. Doses above 1 mg/day are not well-established in human studies. Body weight does not typically dictate injectable dose at these levels.

## Timing & Cycling

For injectable GHK-Cu, most protocols use a once-daily morning injection, though twice-daily splitting is also common. Morning timing is preferred by some because the body's natural tissue repair and gene-expression signaling tends to peak during active daytime hours. Taking it on an empty stomach is not required. For topical GHK-Cu, it is commonly applied once or twice daily — many skin care protocols use it in the evening as part of a recovery routine, often after cleansing and before moisturizer. There is no strong evidence linking workout timing to GHK-Cu effectiveness.

GHK-Cu is commonly cycled using a "5 days on, 2 days off" schedule each week (e.g., Monday through Friday with weekends off). This approach is designed to protect the receptors from becoming desensitized and to prevent diminishing returns over time. Course lengths typically run 8–12 weeks followed by a 2–4 week break. For topical use, continuous daily use is more common and generally considered safe, since topical absorption is relatively low compared to injection. Some longevity-focused protocols pair GHK-Cu with Epithalon in 10–20 day courses a few times per year.

Important: GHK-Cu is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
