## What It Is

FOXO4-DRI (FOXO4 D-Retro-Inverso Peptide) — a specially engineered peptide designed to selectively trigger the self-destruction of 'zombie cells' (old, damaged cells that refuse to die and poison their neighbors)

## What It Does

As cells age and get damaged, some of them go into a kind of 'zombie' state — they stop working properly but refuse to die, and they leak toxic signals that cause inflammation in surrounding tissue. FOXO4-DRI works by cutting a specific handshake between two proteins inside these zombie cells that was keeping them alive. Once that handshake is broken, the zombie cell triggers its own death sequence, clearing it out while leaving healthy cells completely unharmed.

## Potential Benefits

- May selectively destroy damaged 'zombie cells' without harming healthy tissue
- In aging mice, research showed improved physical fitness, thicker fur, and better kidney function
- Could help reduce chronic low-grade inflammation driven by zombie cell secretions
- May improve blood vessel health and slow vascular aging
- Research suggests potential benefits for conditions like joint disease, kidney disease, and lung disease
- Could counter some toxic side effects of chemotherapy drugs

## Where It Came From

Designed by Marjolein Baar, Peter de Keizer, and colleagues at Erasmus University Medical Center in Rotterdam, Netherlands, and first published in the journal Cell in March 2017.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (standard route, most studied)**

All published animal research and the small body of emerging human protocols use subcutaneous injection. The peptide needs to enter the bloodstream to reach senescent ("zombie") cells throughout the body; injection ensures this happens without the unpredictability of oral absorption.

### 💉 Intraperitoneal injection (used in animal studies only)

Some mouse studies injected FOXO4-DRI directly into the abdominal cavity. This route is not used in human settings and is mentioned only for research context.

### 💊 Oral / by mouth (not recommended, no supporting data)

No studies have demonstrated meaningful oral bioavailability for FOXO4-DRI. As a medium-sized peptide, it is expected to be broken down by stomach acid before it can act. No oral form is currently in use in clinical or biohacking contexts.

### 🏥 IV infusion (theoretical, occasionally mentioned)

Intravenous use could in theory deliver the peptide systemically, but there are no human protocols using this route and it carries higher risk.

## Dosage Context

- Important note: FOXO4-DRI has no established, FDA-approved dosing for humans. All protocols below come from animal research and emerging (unvalidated) biohacking protocols.
- Lower end of the reported range: 250 mcg administered per day subcutaneously, titrating up gradually over the first 4 weeks. This is a cautious starting point seen in research-oriented self-administration guidelines.
- Typical range reported in the literature: 250–500 mcg administered per day subcutaneously, used in a gradual 8–16 week course. Animal studies typically used doses of 5 mg/kg every other day intraperitoneally — translating that directly to human self-administration is imprecise and not scientifically validated.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: Up to 500 mcg administered per day for the duration of a 16-week protocol. Some biohacking references mention 1 mg per injection with 2 mL diluent ("1 mg/20" in cheat-sheet shorthand), but this lacks clinical validation. Body weight does influence dosing in the animal studies (per-kg dosing), but human clinical weight-based dosing has not been established.

## Timing & Cycling

In the animal studies and emerging human protocols, FOXO4-DRI is administered once daily in the morning. The morning timing is practical rather than based on a strong circadian rationale. Since senolytic (zombie-cell-clearing) effects are not acute or immediate, timing relative to food or workouts does not appear to be critical. The peptide's mechanism is cumulative over weeks, not hour-by-hour.

FOXO4-DRI is almost universally described as a cycled therapy — not something to take indefinitely every day. The most common emerging protocols run 10–16 weeks, then stop. The rationale is that senolytics (compounds that eliminate senescent cells) are meant to clear a backlog of dysfunctional cells; once that is done, the body's natural mechanisms can largely keep up without ongoing treatment. Running a senolytic continuously could theoretically interfere with normal cellular function. Clinics offering this peptide typically design individualized protocols of 10–30 days at a time, repeated perhaps once or twice per year. There are no large human clinical trials yet confirming the optimal cycle structure.

Important: FOXO4-DRI is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
