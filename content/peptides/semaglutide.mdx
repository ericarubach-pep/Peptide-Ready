## What It Is

Semaglutide — a 31-amino-acid lab-engineered copy of a gut hormone your body naturally makes after eating, modified to last a full week in your body. Sold as Ozempic and Wegovy (injectable) or Rybelsus (pill).

## What It Does

After you eat, your gut releases a hormone called GLP-1 that tells your pancreas to release insulin (to manage blood sugar), tells your stomach to slow down emptying (so you feel full longer), and sends signals to your brain reducing appetite. Semaglutide works exactly the same way — it's a tweaked copy of GLP-1 that lasts about 7 days instead of minutes. A fatty acid chain attached to the molecule lets it hitch a ride on a blood protein, keeping it active for a whole week on a single injection. The brain signal reducing hunger is especially powerful, which is why it produces such dramatic weight loss.

## Potential Benefits

- Significantly lowers blood sugar in type 2 diabetes — better than nearly all competing medications in clinical trials
- May cause an average of about 15% body weight loss in people with obesity (roughly 30–40 lbs for many users)
- Reduces the risk of heart attack and stroke by about 20–26% in high-risk patients
- Could slow kidney disease progression in people with diabetes
- May lower blood pressure, blood fats, and reduce inflammation
- Early research suggests possible brain-protective effects against dementia

## Where It Came From

Developed by Novo Nordisk (Denmark) in 2012 as a longer-lasting follow-up to an earlier drug called liraglutide. FDA approved Ozempic for diabetes in December 2017 and Wegovy for obesity in June 2021. The natural GLP-1 hormone was first identified in the mid-1980s by research groups at Massachusetts General Hospital and the University of Copenhagen.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (most effective, most studied)**

The approved injectable forms (Ozempic for diabetes, Wegovy for weight loss) are injected once weekly under the skin of the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm. Bioavailability is about 89%, meaning nearly all of it gets into the bloodstream. This is the gold standard route with the most clinical data.

### 💊 Oral tablet / by mouth (effective but requires much higher doses)

Rybelsus (oral semaglutide for diabetes) and the newer Wegovy pill are FDA-approved oral forms. The stomach destroys most of the drug, so oral doses must be much higher (3–25 mg administered daily) compared to injectable doses (0.25–7.2 mg administered weekly). Must be taken on an empty stomach with a small amount of water to maximize what little gets absorbed through the gut lining.

### 🏥 Intravenous / IV (not used clinically)

Not an approved or practical route. The subcutaneous injection works well and there is no benefit to IV for this drug.

## Dosage Context

- Injectable form (Ozempic/Wegovy):
- Lower end of the reported range: 0.25 mg once weekly for the first 4 weeks. This is described as an adjustment-phase dose rather than a therapeutic dose — it minimizes nausea and digestive side effects.
- Typical range reported in the literature: 0.5–2.4 mg once weekly. For diabetes (Ozempic), the typical maintenance dose is 0.5–2 mg administered weekly. For weight loss (Wegovy), the target maintenance dose is 2.4 mg administered weekly after a gradual dose escalation over 4–5 months.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: Up to 7.2 mg once weekly (FDA approved for Wegovy as of March 2026 for weight loss). Escalation to this dose requires at least 4 weeks at the 2.4 mg dose first.
- Oral form (Rybelsus):
- Starting: 3 mg administered daily for 30 days.
- Typical: 7–14 mg administered daily.
- High: 25 mg administered daily (approved for Wegovy pill).
- Dose is not typically adjusted by body weight for the standard escalation schedule.

## Timing & Cycling

Injectable: Once weekly, on the same day each week. It can be taken at any time of day, with or without food. Injection sites are typically rotated each week. For best long-term adherence, a day and time that is easy to remember is typically chosen and used consistently — the day of the week is described as mattering more than the hour.

Oral: First-thing-in-the-morning dosing on an empty stomach, with no more than 4 oz of plain water, is standard for the oral form. A wait of at least 30 minutes before eating, drinking anything else, or taking other medications is typically observed. Food dramatically reduces absorption of the oral form.

Semaglutide is designed for continuous long-term use, not cycling. It is a chronic disease management drug — like blood pressure or cholesterol medication. The weight loss and blood sugar control it provides largely disappear within weeks to months of stopping, as the drug leaves the system and appetite returns. Clinical guidelines treat it as an ongoing therapy that continues as long as it is needed and tolerated. Stopping and restarting does work, but there is no clinical benefit to intentional breaks (cycling) — they simply allow the beneficial effects to reverse. Dose adjustments for side effects are common, but 'cycling off' is not a recommended strategy.

Important: Semaglutide is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
