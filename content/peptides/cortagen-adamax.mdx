## What It Is

Cortagen / Adamax (AEDP) — a 4-amino-acid synthetic peptide (derived from analyzing the natural peptide blend found in bovine brain cortex tissue, and developed as a nerve-protective and brain-supportive compound)

## What It Does

Like several other peptides in the Khavinson family, Cortagen is small enough to slip directly into the cell nucleus and interact with the genetic material there, acting like a dimmer switch that adjusts which genes are active. In nerve tissue, it turns up genes responsible for growing and repairing nerves and turns down genes that trigger cell death — helping damaged neurons recover. It also supports heart cell function by adjusting genes involved in how the heart manages energy and electrical signaling.

## Potential Benefits

- Research in animals shows it can support structural and functional recovery of damaged peripheral nerves, including full sciatic nerve regeneration
- May protect brain cells and support cognitive function, including spatial memory and learning
- Could have cardiovascular benefits by modulating how heart cells regulate their activity
- Research suggests antioxidant and anti-aging properties
- May be relevant for recovery from brain injury, poor brain circulation, and stroke-related damage
- Could support nerve recovery in peripheral neuropathy (nerve damage in the limbs)

## Where It Came From

Developed in Russia at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology by Professor Vladimir Khavinson and colleagues in the late 1990s–early 2000s, created by chemically synthesizing a peptide whose structure was deduced from analyzing an existing natural brain extract called Cortexin that had been used clinically in Russia for years.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

### CORTAGEN

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (most studied in animal research)**

Cortagen animal studies (rats, mice) used subcutaneous or intraperitoneal injection. Subcutaneous is the most practical and most commonly cited route in human-directed protocols. Delivers the peptide directly into the bloodstream for systemic brain effects.

### 💊 Oral tablets / capsules (used in Russian bioregulator tradition)

Cortagen is sold in some markets (particularly Eastern European) as oral tablets (1 mg per tablet). It is a very short tetrapeptide (4 amino acids), which may allow partial oral bioavailability, similar to other small bioregulator peptides. Some research sources note no published oral bioavailability data exists, while others describe oral use as part of the established bioregulator protocol.

### 👃 Intranasal (nasal spray – studied in animal models, limited human data)

Intranasal delivery has been explored for Cortagen and similar brain-targeting bioregulators. The nasal-to-brain pathway could enhance CNS delivery.

### ADAMAX (N-Acetyl Semax Amidate with adamantane modification)

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (primary route, superior potency)**

Adamax is an adamantane-modified analog of Semax with significantly enhanced blood-brain barrier penetration and peptide stability. Subcutaneous injection is the established route for research protocols. Unlike plain Semax, intranasal Adamax data is limited; the adamantane modification may make the molecule behave differently in the nasal passage.

### 👃 Nasal spray (less data than plain Semax, may still work)

Used by some, but the adamantane modification adds a bulky chemical group that may reduce intranasal absorption efficiency compared to regular Semax.

## Dosage Context

**CORTAGEN**

- Lower end of the reported range: 0.1–0.2 mg administered per day subcutaneously, or 1 mg per oral tablet once daily. A weight-based starting point is approximately 10 mcg/kg per day (i.e., 700 mcg for a 70 kg person).
- Typical range reported in the literature: 0.5–1 mg administered per day. For those under 100 kg: 1 mg/day (one oral tablet or one 1 mg injection). For those over 100 kg: up to 2 mg/day. Some bioregulator protocols list 5–10 mg administered per day (5–10 oral tablets) for more intensive support.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: Up to 2 mg/day injectable or 10 mg/day oral tablets in intensive protocols. Note: no validated human clinical dosing exists — all figures are extrapolated from animal research or empirical bioregulator protocols.

**ADAMAX**

- Lower end of the reported range: 300 mcg administered per day subcutaneously (weeks 1–2 of a gradual titration).
- Typical range reported in the literature: 500–750 mcg administered per day subcutaneously (weeks 3–6).
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: 1,000 mcg (1 mg) per day subcutaneously (weeks 7–8+). Adamax is considered significantly more potent per microgram than standard Semax, so doses are kept lower.

## Timing & Cycling

CORTAGEN: Typically taken in the morning. Short treatment courses (10–30 days) are the norm, often following the "Khavinsky longevity bioregulator protocol" which pairs Cortagen with Pinealon for a combined hippocampal-cortical neuroprotection stack. No food timing requirement.

ADAMAX: Once-daily subcutaneous injection in the morning. Like Semax, Adamax has activating cognitive effects; avoid evening dosing to prevent sleep disruption. No fasting required, though some users prefer morning on an empty stomach.

CORTAGEN: Follows the classic Russian bioregulator cycling model — 10–30 day courses, 2–3 times per year, with breaks of several months in between. The effects are structural and gene-expression-level, not acute. No evidence supports continuous daily year-round use. Some practitioners run Cortagen and Pinealon together in the same cycle; others alternate them.

ADAMAX: Cycled in 8–16 week courses followed by a rest period (typically 4 weeks off). This mirrors the Semax cycling protocol from which Adamax is derived. Continuous indefinite use is not recommended due to the CNS-stimulating nature of the peptide and the desire to maintain receptor sensitivity over time.

Important: Cortagen / Adamax (AEDP) is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
