## What It Is

Mazdutide (also called IBI362) — a lab-made peptide that activates two separate hormone receptors at once (the receptors for GLP-1, a gut satiety hormone, and glucagon, a liver energy-release hormone)

## What It Does

Mazdutide hits two targets simultaneously, which is what makes it more powerful than older weight-loss drugs that only hit one. The first target slows digestion, makes you feel full faster, lowers blood sugar, and tells your brain to eat less. The second target tells your body to burn more fat for energy — essentially revving up your metabolism — and directly reduces fat accumulation in the liver. Together, these two effects produce greater weight loss than either action alone.

## Potential Benefits

- In clinical trials, produced an average of 10–11% body weight reduction at higher doses
- Outperformed semaglutide (Ozempic) head-to-head in a Phase 3 trial for combined blood sugar control and weight loss
- Could significantly reduce blood sugar levels in people with type 2 diabetes
- May reduce dangerous fat buildup in the liver
- Research suggests improvements in cholesterol, blood pressure, and body composition
- Approved in China as the world's first GLP-1/glucagon dual receptor agonist for weight management and type 2 diabetes

## Where It Came From

Developed by Innovent Biologics in China in collaboration with Eli Lilly, based on a natural gut peptide called oxyntomodulin that activates both target receptors; clinical trials began around 2018–2019.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

**💉 1. Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (only established route) — All clinical trials and its approved use (as of 2025 in China) use once-weekly subcutaneous injection. Like other GLP-1/GCG dual agonists, Mazdutide is a large peptide that cannot survive oral digestion, and it is not available as a nasal spray or IV formulation. Injection is performed in the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm.**

**🏥 Note: Mazdutide received regulatory approval in China for chronic weight management in 2025. It is not FDA-approved in the United States as of this writing and is not available through standard Western pharmacies or compounding channels.**

## Dosage Context

- Lower end of the reported range: 3 mg subcutaneously, once per week. This is the lowest dose tested in Phase 2 trials (NCT04904913) and the recommended starting point to minimize gastrointestinal side effects (nausea, vomiting).
- Typical range reported in the literature: 4.5–6 mg once per week subcutaneously. Phase 2 trials showed 4.5 mg and 6 mg produced clinically meaningful weight loss (roughly 6–7% of body weight at 24 weeks) with manageable side effects in Chinese adults.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: 9 mg once per week (Phase 3 GLORY trials). The GLORY-2 trial showed 9 mg achieved up to 20% mean body weight reduction at 60 weeks in people with obesity. Higher doses (9 mg) are associated with greater weight loss but also more gastrointestinal side effects. Dose titration is done gradually over weeks — starting at 3 mg and stepping up to 4.5 mg, then 6 mg, at 4-week intervals as tolerated. Body weight does not change the dose, but BMI and comorbidities influence clinical decision-making.

## Timing & Cycling

Mazdutide is injected once per week, on the same day each week, at any time of day. It can be taken with or without food. Because it is a once-weekly injection with a long half-life, meal timing around the injection does not significantly affect results. Most patients pick a consistent day of the week (e.g., every Monday) and administer via injection in the abdomen or thigh.

Mazdutide follows the same long-term continuous-use model as other GLP-1-class drugs (like semaglutide / Ozempic or tirzepatide / Mounjaro) — it is designed to be taken indefinitely, not cycled. Weight regain after stopping is a well-documented phenomenon with GLP-1 receptor agonists; most of the weight lost returns within 12 months of discontinuation. The drug is meant to treat obesity as a chronic condition requiring ongoing management. Patients typically remain on the medication as long as it is effective and tolerated, under medical supervision.

Important: Mazdutide is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
