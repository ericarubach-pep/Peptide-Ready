## What It Is

Thymalin — a complex mixture of short peptides (chains of 2–3 amino acids) extracted from the thymus gland of calves, with the key active pieces being two tiny dipeptides called KE and EW

## What It Does

The thymus is a gland in your chest that trains your immune cells — it's like a boot camp for the white blood cells that fight infections and cancer. Thymalin's small peptide pieces slip into your cells and interact directly with DNA, dialing up the production of immune-system proteins. It helps immature immune stem cells grow up into functional disease-fighting cells, and it dials down the runaway inflammatory signals (called cytokines) that can cause your immune system to go haywire. Think of it as a tune-up for an aging or underperforming immune system.

## Potential Benefits

- Research suggests it can cut hospital death rates in severely ill elderly patients with infections like COVID-19
- May help prevent the immune system from overreacting ("cytokine storm")
- Could restore normal immune cell balance in people with weakened immunity
- Research in older adults suggests it may lower rates of heart disease, high blood pressure, bone loss, and respiratory infections
- Animal studies show potential lifespan extension when combined with other peptides
- May have anti-tumor effects based on early lab research

## Where It Came From

First extracted from calf thymus glands and characterized in 1977 by Vladimir Khavinson and Vyacheslav Morozov at the Military Medical Academy and St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology in Russia, as part of a large program studying age-related immune decline.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

**💉 Intramuscular injection / into the muscle (most effective, most studied)**

Clinical studies, including a COVID-19 trial, used 10 mg intramuscularly daily for 10 days. This route delivers Thymalin directly into muscle tissue for reliable, fast absorption into the bloodstream. It is the historically preferred clinical route.

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (effective, commonly used alternative)**

Subcutaneous injection is widely used in practice as a more convenient self-administration option. Absorption is slightly slower than intramuscular but still effective, and it avoids the discomfort of deeper muscle injections.

## Dosage Context

- Lower end of the reported range: 5 mg administered daily for a course of 5–10 consecutive days. This is the lower end of the clinical range and a reasonable starting point.
- Typical range reported in the literature: 5–10 mg administered daily for 5–10 consecutive days. This matches clinical trial protocols (e.g., 10 mg/day intramuscularly for 10 days as used in COVID-19 research). Many protocols use this as a short 'course' rather than ongoing daily use.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: 10 mg administered daily for up to 10 days is at the upper end of established practice. Going beyond this is not backed by clinical data and is not recommended.
- Dosage does not typically vary by body weight in the protocols studied.

## Timing & Cycling

Once-daily administration, typically in the morning or early evening, is standard. Thymalin does not have stimulant effects, so exact time-of-day is flexible, but consistency matters. Aligning with the morning is practical and mirrors how clinical studies were run. It is usually given as a concentrated 'course' — for example, once daily for 5–10 consecutive days — rather than spread out over weeks.

Thymalin is not taken continuously. It is designed as a short course repeated a few times per year. The standard approach is 1–4 courses per year, each course lasting 5–10 consecutive days, with months between courses. For general immune support or anti-aging, 1–2 courses per year is common. For recovery from illness or during high-stress periods, up to 4 courses annually may be used. This pulsed cycling approach mimics how the thymus gland naturally provides immune support and avoids overstimulation of the immune system.

Important: Thymalin is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
