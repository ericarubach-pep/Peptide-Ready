## What It Is

Pinealon (EDR) — a 3-amino-acid peptide (Glutamic acid–Aspartic acid–Arginine) originally isolated from brain and pineal gland tissue, developed as a neuroprotective compound

## What It Does

Pinealon is tiny enough to pass through cell walls and even reach the nucleus — the command center of the cell — where it can directly influence which genes get turned on or off. Inside neurons (brain cells), it activates survival genes, boosts the production of brain growth factors that help neurons stay healthy and connected, and dials down the chemical signals that cause neurons to over-fire and self-destruct. It also increases the brain's own antioxidant defenses and supports the production of serotonin, a chemical tied to mood balance.

## Potential Benefits

- May protect brain cells from damage caused by low oxygen, oxidative stress, or reduced blood flow
- Research suggests it could protect the tiny branching connections between neurons that are lost in Alzheimer's disease
- Could improve memory and learning, based on animal studies
- May support mood regulation through effects on serotonin production
- Research suggests potential anti-aging effects by reducing markers of cellular aging in the brain
- Could benefit cognitive performance in conditions involving poor circulation or high blood sugar

## Where It Came From

Developed in Saint Petersburg, Russia, in the early 2000s (approximately 2001) by Professor Vladimir Khavinson and colleagues at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology, as part of a decades-long program of isolating protective peptides from specific body tissues.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (most effective for systemic and neuroprotective effects)**

Used in physician-supervised compounding protocols. Delivers Pinealon directly into the bloodstream, allowing it to cross the blood-brain barrier and reach neuronal cells. This is the preferred route in Western compounding/longevity clinic settings.

### 💊 Oral / by mouth (used in some Russian research contexts)

Pinealon's original research was conducted in Russia where oral administration has been described in clinical contexts. The EDR tripeptide is small and may have partial resistance to digestive breakdown, similar to other bioregulator peptides in this family. However, oral bioavailability is considered lower and less predictable than injection.

### 👃 Nasal spray (emerging, limited data)

Some compounding pharmacies offer nasal formulations. The nasal route could theoretically improve brain targeting, but well-controlled data on Pinealon via this route is lacking.

## Dosage Context

- Lower end of the reported range: 1 mg administered per day subcutaneously (the standard initial range cited by innerbody.com and physician protocols). Some gradual-approach research references start as low as 200–500 mcg administered per day for the first 2 weeks.
- Typical range reported in the literature: 1–5 mg administered per day subcutaneously in a physician-supervised context. Clinical compounding settings most commonly reference 5 mg per injection once daily during an active cycle.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: Up to 10 mg administered per day for intensive neuroprotective support under close physician monitoring. Some oral tablet forms (in Russian-style bioregulator protocols) are sold in 1 mg tablets at 5–10 mg administered per day. Body weight does not typically dictate dosing in standard protocols.

## Timing & Cycling

Morning or early afternoon administration is most commonly recommended, timed to align with the brain's active-state neuroplasticity and mitochondrial activity. Unlike Epithalon (which is sometimes timed with evening melatonin patterns), Pinealon's cognitive and neuroprotective effects are thought to be better supported during waking hours. A 5-days-on, 2-days-off weekly schedule (a common bioregulator approach) or an active-cycle approach of 10–20 consecutive days are both described in the source material. No food timing requirement.

Pinealon follows the classic Russian bioregulator cycling model: 10–20 day active course, then a break of several months, with 2–3 courses per year. This is consistent with how Epithalon and Cortagen (from the same research tradition) are used. The effects are cumulative and structural at the gene-expression level — immediate day-to-day changes are not typically reported, with benefit described as building over weeks to months. Continuous daily use year-round is not supported and is considered unnecessary. Many longevity practitioners pair Pinealon with Epithalon in the same course, then alternate with other bioregulators.

Important: Pinealon (EDR) is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
