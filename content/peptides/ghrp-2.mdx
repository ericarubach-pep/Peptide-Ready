## What It Is

GHRP-2 (Growth Hormone-Releasing Peptide-2; also called Pralmorelin) — a 6-amino-acid synthetic peptide that mimics ghrelin (your body's "hunger hormone") to trigger growth hormone release. Approved in Japan as a diagnostic tool for testing growth hormone deficiency.

## What It Does

Ghrelin is a hormone made in your stomach when you're hungry — among other things it tells your brain to release growth hormone. GHRP-2 is a lab-made molecule that activates the exact same receiving station as ghrelin (called the GHS-R1a receptor) on your pituitary gland, triggering a surge of growth hormone. But it also acts on a second receptor found on heart cells (called CD36), activating protective pathways that reduce cell death and inflammation in the heart and liver. One side effect is that it also increases appetite — unlike its more selective cousin ipamorelin — and can mildly raise cortisol (your stress hormone).

## Potential Benefits

- Reliably triggers growth hormone release, useful for both clinical testing and body composition goals
- Approved in Japan as a diagnostic tool to test whether someone's pituitary gland can produce growth hormone
- Animal research shows strong heart protection — reduced heart cell death and improved heart function after heart attacks
- May protect the liver from inflammation and scarring based on animal studies
- Research suggests it could help prevent muscle wasting
- Could reduce the buildup of fatty plaques in arteries based on animal studies

## Where It Came From

Developed by Cyril Y. Bowers at Tulane University (New Orleans, USA), the pioneering scientist who invented the entire family of growth hormone-releasing peptides. Bowers first noticed in the early 1980s that certain chemical tweaks to pain-modulating compounds could trigger growth hormone release from pituitary cells — GHRP-6 came first around 1984, followed by GHRP-2. This research ultimately led to the discovery of ghrelin itself in Japan in 1999.

## How It's Taken

Here's a breakdown of the available delivery methods and how they compare:

**💉 Injection under the skin / subcutaneous (most common and practical)**

The standard route for GHRP-2 in clinical and research use. Produces peak GH levels within 15–30 minutes of injection. Easy to self-administer into abdominal fat, thigh, or upper arm.

### 💉 Intravenous / IV (most potent, research context)

IV injection produces the fastest and strongest GH pulse. Used in clinical research comparisons. A single 1 mcg/kg IV dose of GHRP-2 produced GH responses exceeding those from maximally effective GHRH in studies. Not for routine self-use.

### 👃 Intranasal / nasal spray (less effective)

GHRP-2 retains some GH-releasing activity when administered intranasally, but the dose absorbed through the nasal lining is significantly lower than with injection. Some compounded nasal sprays are available but are considered inferior to subcutaneous delivery.

### 💊 Oral / by mouth (poor effectiveness)

Some limited GH-releasing activity has been detected after oral GHRP-2 administration, but digestion degrades most of the peptide. Not a practical route.

## Dosage Context

- Lower end of the reported range: 100 mcg subcutaneously once daily before bed, for 8–12 weeks. The bedtime dose captures the body's largest natural GH pulse during sleep.
- Typical range reported in the literature: 100–200 mcg per injection, 2 times daily (morning fasted and before bed), for 8–12 weeks. This is the most common research and wellness protocol.
- Higher end of the range documented in protocols: 200–300 mcg per injection, 2–3 times daily, often combined with a GHRH analog like CJC-1295 (no DAC) for synergistic GH output, for 8–16 weeks. The GH response plateaus at approximately 1–2 mcg/kg per injection — higher doses do not produce more GH per shot.
- Weight-based saturation dose: approximately 1–2 mcg per kg of body weight per injection. For a 80 kg person, that is 80–160 mcg as the effective range.

## Timing & Cycling

Dosing on an empty stomach is standard — with at least a 2-hour wait after eating, and roughly 20–30 minutes further avoided before eating after the dose. Elevated blood glucose and insulin significantly blunt the GH response. Best injection windows are: (1) morning fasted, (2) pre-workout, (3) before bed (the most important window, as it amplifies the natural overnight GH surge). For twice-daily dosing, injections are typically spaced at least 6–8 hours apart. High-carbohydrate or high-fat meals around dosing times are generally avoided.

GHRP-2 requires cycling to maintain receptor sensitivity. The pituitary growth hormone secretagogue receptor (GHSR) can become desensitized with continuous daily use. One study noted a reduction in GH response after 5 consecutive days of GHRP-2, reinforcing the need for breaks. Standard cycle: 8–12 weeks on, followed by 4–8 weeks off. Some practitioners use a 5-days-on / 2-days-off weekly pattern within a cycle as an added precaution. Long-term continuous use is not recommended. GHRP-2 is often combined with a GHRH analog; when stacking, both peptides are cycled together.

Important: GHRP-2 is not FDA-approved for human use as a research peptide and remains under investigation. All human use is off-label. Physician consultation before use in any form is the standard recommendation across these source materials.
